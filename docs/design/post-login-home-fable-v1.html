The line is the page.

The first mock laid the home out as a dashboard of cards. This one takes the brand's own idea literally: one route runs down the page, each stop opens where it sits, and the stop you are at is the only thing that asks for attention. Everything else is a quiet rail on the right. Same tokens, fonts and icons as the live app.

Compared with the first design (v1, the card dashboard)

Fewer boxes, one spine.

Both mocks show the same blocks and use the same components and tables, so the v1 build table still applies. The difference is composition.

Structure

  • No sidebar. The five steps are not navigation; they are the page. Top nav carries only Home, Library, Resume builder and Network; Profile sits under the avatar.
  • One card. Only the current stop is boxed. Done stops collapse to a line; future stops are grey and say when they open.
  • Junction. The posting board Milin asked for on 8 Oct: posts with votes, comments, reshares and Save; types Idea, Question, Win, Resource; filters Top, New, My route, My interests, Following; interests are anything a person wants to learn or talk about, job-related or not, and tag both posts and the feed; Report on every post. Once users post, the app is an intermediary under India's IT Rules 2021: a named grievance officer, complaints answered in 24 to 72 hours, and someone moderating. The rail shows where that lives.
  • People and profiles. A person in any list shows only two numbers: shared skills and shared interests. Opening the profile shows what you have in common (interests, skills, gaps and where each of you is on the path, people in common) plus their public fields: certifications and Junction posts. Private fields never appear.
  • How people appear. In every list (requests, suggestions, connections) a person shows as their name plus a status they set themselves: Working at a company, Freelance, Looking for a next role, Studying, or none. Names are what people search by; the handle stays on the profile and its link.
  • Rail, not grid. Profile, Library, Builder and People are one narrow column separated by rules, 320px on desktop, stacked under the line on a phone.

Where this line view does better

  • The eye lands on one action, Mark done, and nothing competes with it.
  • The greeting says where you are in words ("at Path, step 4 of 9"), so the route needs no separate stepper widget.
  • It scales: when Practice and Match launch, they fill in where they already stand, with no layout change.

Where the first design (cards) does better

  • The dashboard shows the portal (Library, Builder, Network) at equal weight; here they are secondary by design.
  • v1 handles first sign-in and no-resume states in place; here the line would simply start at Resume with an upload prompt at that stop (not drawn).
  • A sidebar is what most people expect from an app shell; a top nav is lighter but holds fewer items.
Recommendation

Build the line view, and borrow three things from v1.

  1. Keep this layout for Home: the route down the page, one card for the current step, the quiet rail, plus Network, Junction and profiles as drawn here.
  2. Take v1's first sign-in and no-resume states. In the line view they become the Resume stop: a setup checklist after first sign-in, an upload prompt when there is no resume yet.
  3. Take v1's build table (which component and data each block uses) as the developer's checklist.
  4. Add a "More" menu to the top bar if it grows past five items, so the lighter nav never runs out of room.
