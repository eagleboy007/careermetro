Option B

The board flips to your next stop.

Option A draws the journey. Option B is the moment of arrival: one tile of a station departures board, caught mid-flip. The top half is today in ink, the bottom half has already turned cobalt. The letter is the c of careermetro, so the icon is also our initial.

01 · Mark

One tile, two halves

It's a square split by the seam every split-flap display has, so it works where square marks live: app icons, profile photos, browser tabs.

{{MARK_BIG}}
On the website the top flap can drop once, on first load.
  • 1
    Ink top: today. Where your resume puts you now.
  • 2
    The seam: the flip. Proof is what turns the board. A course doesn't flip it, a passed skill check or a certificate does.
  • 3
    Cobalt bottom: the next stop. Cobalt still means progress, as it does everywhere in the product.
  • 4
    The c. Our initial, set in Bricolage Grotesque Bold, the same face as the headings.

04 · Compare

A or B

Both use the same colours and the same rule that cobalt means progress. Either can launch.

A · the lineB · the board
StoryThe whole journey: where you are, the climb, the destination. It can grow into the visual flow and the map.The moment you arrive. It ties to the Departures and Arrivals screens.
Small sizesGood down to 24 px. At 16 px it reads as a tick.The icon is strongest: a letter in a tile reads at any size. The wordmark is weaker, because below about 20 px the seam can look like crossed-out text.
OwnableLine-and-dot marks are common in career and analytics apps.Letter tiles are common. The seam and the ink-to-cobalt split are what make it ours, so it needs the same trade mark search.
MotionThe line draws itself.The tile flips, and the wordmark can flip letter by letter.
Feels likeCalm, product, trustworthy.Louder and more playful, closer to a consumer app.
MY PICK

A as the logo, because it is the product's idea and it doubles as your visual flow. If you prefer B, the strongest use of it is as the app icon and the motion on the Departures screen, with A's wordmark beside it. Mixing them in one lockup would be one idea too many.
